## First-page screen

The title names the mechanism and the almost-all Collatz conclusion.  The
abstract states:

- the exact shortcut map;
- natural density rather than logarithmic density;
- every fixed strict endpoint \(0<\delta<1\);
- the quantitative exceptional count;
- the \(6.953\log n\) shortcut clock;
- the optional orbit ceiling.

The first two pages make the shortcut/raw clock convention explicit and state
that \(\delta=1\) is not claimed.

## Baseline and novelty screen

The comparison table prevents the most serious editorial overclaim.  It says
that the 2026 Allikvere and Mazur manuscripts have more general
arbitrary-diverging targets and that Inselmann already reaches the same
shortcut-time scale for fixed power targets.  The present paper is positioned
instead around:

1. a 14-page self-contained proof;
2. a stretched-logarithmic natural-density target;
3. a target-specific stretched-exponential exceptional rate;
4. an arbitrary-target pointwise first-passage transport interface.

This is a credible multi-axis contribution, not a claim of total dominance.

## Presentation risks

1. Any cover letter that calls the theorem the strongest natural-density
   Collatz result would contradict the paper's own comparison table.
2. Because the proof is unexpectedly elementary for a strong Collatz
   statement, a handling editor is likely to inspect Proposition 4.4 and
   Theorem 5.1 before sending the paper out.
3. The exact novelty of Proposition 4.4 has not received an exhaustive
   historical priority search.

The cleanest cover-letter sentence is:

> The paper gives a self-contained first-passage proof of natural-density
> stretched-logarithmic descent with a quantitative exceptional rate; it is
> complementary to, and weaker in target generality than, recent
> arbitrary-threshold preprints.
